How it works

We reverse-engineered the viral “villager sings” covers. They aren’t villager sound clips glued onto notes: the singer’s own vocal is re-voiced by a model trained on villager sounds, so the phrasing stays human and the consonants come out as villager hit sounds.

  1. Find the hook

    For a 30-second cover, the loudest stretch of the song whose harmony repeats most often is picked and snapped to a beat. That is almost always the chorus.

  2. Separate

    Mel-Band RoFormer splits the track into the full vocal (lead and backing) and the instrumental.

  3. Villagerize

    An RVC villager voice re-sings the vocal 8 semitones lower, or an octave lower if you want it in key. Pitch, timing and loudness follow the original singer.

  4. Mix and master

    The villager sits dry and centred, about 4 dB under the original music, with the rumble cut and the top end softened, then loudness-matched to −16 LUFS.

Villager Sound is a fan project and is not affiliated with Mojang or Microsoft. Voice models are third-party; check their licences before publishing covers, and respect the rights of the songs you use.
